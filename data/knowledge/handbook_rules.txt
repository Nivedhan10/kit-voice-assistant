1.1
Purpose
1.1.1 This handbook sets out the academic regulations of Knotopian Institute of Technology (KIT) for
the Academic Year 2026–27. It covers attendance, leave, on-duty, examinations, fees, placements,
conduct and the services available to students.
1.1.2 Every student is expected to read the chapters that apply to their programme. Ignorance of a
rule is not accepted as a reason for an exception.
1.1.3 Where this handbook says "the college system", it means the KIT online academic system
through which students apply for leave and on-duty, view attendance and timetables, and register for
placement drives, and through which faculty members approve or reject requests.
1.2
Who
the
rules
apply
to
1.2.1 Chapters 4, 6 and 10 apply only to undergraduate (B.Tech) students. Chapters 5, 7 and 11 apply
only to postgraduate (M.Tech) students. All other chapters apply to both.
1.2.2 The undergraduate and postgraduate chapters are written in a similar order so that students can
compare them, but the numbers in them are different. A student must always read the chapter for
their own programme. For example, the minimum attendance for B.Tech students is in Rule 4.1.1 and
the minimum attendance for M.Tech students is in Rule 5.1.1; they are not the same.
1.2.3 Lateral changes between programmes, readmission after a break of study and transfers from
other institutions are governed by separate regulations issued by the Office of the Dean (Academics)
and are not covered here.
1.3
Structure
of
the
rules
1.3.1 Each rule has a number made of the chapter, the section and the rule, for example Rule 4.3.2 is
the second rule of section 3 of Chapter 4. Tables are numbered after the section in which they appear,
for example Table 4.4.
1.3.2 Where a rule refers to another rule, the words "see" or "under" are followed by the rule number.
The referred rule applies in full.
1.3.3 Examples given in this handbook are for explanation only. They use made-up numbers and do
not describe any particular student.
1.4
Circulars
and
the
order
of
precedence
1.4.1 After this handbook is published, rules are changed or added only through numbered circulars
issued by the Principal, the Dean (Academics), the Controller of Examinations or the Head of a
Department within their authority. Circulars are numbered in the form NN/YYYY, for example
Circular 12/2026. Department notices are numbered in the form DEPT/YYYY/NN.

1.4.2 Every circular states its date, its effective date and the students or classes it applies to.
1.4.3 Where a circular and this handbook differ, the circular prevails from its effective date for the
students it applies to. Where two circulars differ, the one with the later effective date prevails.
1.4.4 Proposals, drafts, minutes of student bodies and documents marked "draft" or "not adopted"
have no effect, even when they are printed in or attached to this handbook. Only rules approved by the
Academic Council and circulars issued under Rule 1.4.1 bind the college or its students.
1.4.5 Circulars are published in the college system and on the notice boards. A circular is binding
from its effective date whether or not a student has read it.
1.5
Getting
help
1.5.1 Questions about this handbook should first be raised with the Class Advisor (for B.Tech
students) or the Programme Coordinator (for M.Tech students). Contacts are listed in Chapter 14.
1.5.2 Where a matter is not covered by this handbook or by a circular, the decision of the Principal is
final. Staff and students should not assume a rule exists where none is written.

In this handbook, unless the context requires otherwise, the following words have the meanings given
below.
2.1
Academic
terms
2.1.1
Academic
Year. The period from 1 July to 30 June. The Academic Year 2026–27 runs from 1
July 2026 to 30 June 2027.
2.1.2
Semester. One half of the Academic Year. The odd semester runs from July to November; the
even semester runs from December to April. The dates of each semester are published in the college
calendar.
2.1.3
Course. A subject with its own course code, credits and assessment, for example CS304
Database Management Systems. Attendance is counted separately for each course.
2.1.4
Period. One teaching slot of 50 minutes in the timetable. A course may have one or more
periods on a day.
2.1.5
Periods
held. The number of periods of a course actually conducted from the start of the
semester up to and including a given date. Periods cancelled by the college (for example on holidays)
are not periods held.
2.1.6
Working
day. A day on which classes are held under the timetable. Monday to Friday are
working days unless declared holidays. Saturdays and Sundays are not working days, except a
Saturday declared a working day by circular (see Rule 3.4.2).
2.1.7
Class. A group of students of the same programme, department, year and section, for example
CSE-2A (B.Tech Computer Science and Engineering, second year, section A).

6 or Chapter 7. Sanctioned leave excuses the absence for discipline purposes, but the periods missed
are still counted as absent for attendance (see Rule 4.2.2).
2.2.3
On-duty

of the college, such as a technical symposium, a paper presentation, a sports event or a placement
interview. Periods on approved on-duty count as attended (see Rule 4.2.1).
2.2.4

absence. Absence from a period without sanctioned leave or approved on-duty.

day for personal reasons without applying for leave. Students sometimes call this "bunking" or

3.1
Programmes
3.1.1 The B.Tech programmes run for eight semesters (four years). The college offers B.Tech in
Computer Science and Engineering (CSE), Electronics and Communication Engineering (ECE) and
Mechanical Engineering (MECH).
3.1.2 The M.Tech programme in Computer Science and Engineering runs for four semesters (two
years).
3.1.3 Each semester has between five and seven theory courses and up to three laboratory courses, as
set out in the curriculum of the programme.
3.2
Timetable
3.2.1 Each class has a weekly timetable showing, for each weekday, the periods and the course taught
in each period. The timetable is available in the college system.
3.2.2 Periods run as follows: Period 1, 9:00 to 9:50; Period 2, 10:00 to 10:50; Period 3, 11:00 to 11:50;
Period 4, 12:00 to 12:50; Period 5, 1:40 to 2:30; Period 6, 2:40 to 3:30. Not every class has a period in
every slot.
3.2.3 A change to a class timetable for the rest of the semester is made by department notice. A
change for a single day is made by circular (see section 3.4).
3.3
Calendar
and
holidays
3.3.1 The college calendar for each semester shows the first and last working days, the holidays, the
Internal Assessment dates and the End Semester Examination period. It is published in the college
system before the semester starts.
3.3.2 Holidays declared by the State Government are holidays at the college unless a circular says
otherwise.
3.3.3 Sundays are always holidays.
3.4
Working
Saturdays
and
compensatory
working
days
3.4.1 Saturdays are normally holidays.
3.4.2 To make up for working days lost to unplanned closures, the Principal may declare a Saturday a
working day by circular. The circular names the weekday whose timetable is followed on that
Saturday, for example "Saturday will follow Monday's timetable".
3.4.3 On a declared working Saturday, every class follows the timetable of the named weekday in full.
The periods held on that Saturday are periods held for attendance under Rule 4.2.4, and absence on
that day is treated like absence on any other working day, including under Rule 4.3.2.

Chapter

Attendance
rules
for
undergraduate
(B.Tech)
students
This chapter applies only to B.Tech students. The rules for M.Tech students are in Chapter 5 and use
different numbers.
4.1
Minimum
attendance
for
B.Tech
students
4.1.1 A B.Tech student must have at least 75% attendance in each course, worked out under section
4.2, to be eligible to write the End Semester Examination in that course.
4.1.2 The minimum applies to each course separately. A good overall attendance does not make up for
low attendance in one course. For example, a student with 90% overall attendance but 72% in one
course does not meet Rule 4.1.1 in that course.
4.1.3 Overall attendance (all courses together) is shown in the college system for information only.
Eligibility is always decided course by course.
4.1.4 Attendance is final on the last working day of the semester. A student below 75% in a course on
that day may apply for condonation under section 4.4 if they are within the condonable band;
otherwise section 4.5 applies.
4.1.5 Laboratory courses require at least 80% attendance. Attendance shortage in a laboratory course
cannot be condoned; the student must complete the missed experiments in the make-up sessions
announced by the department before the End Semester Examination.
4.2
How
attendance
is
calculated
4.2.1 Attendance in a course is worked out as follows and rounded to one decimal place:
Attendance % = (periods attended + periods on approved on-duty) ÷ periods held to date × 100
4.2.2 Periods missed on sanctioned personal leave, half-day leave or medical leave are counted as
absent. Sanctioned leave excuses the absence for discipline purposes only. Medical leave can support
an application for condonation under section 4.4 but does not change the percentage.
4.2.3 To work out the effect of planned leave on attendance, add the periods of the course scheduled
on each leave day to the periods held; the periods attended stay the same. No other future periods are
added. Example (made-up numbers): a student has attended 45 of 58 periods held (77.6%). The
course has two periods on the planned leave day. After the leave the student would have 45 of 60,
which is 75.0%.
4.2.4 On a Saturday declared a working day under Rule 3.4.2, the periods of the weekday named in
the circular are held. They are counted as periods held, and a student absent on that day is marked
absent for each of those periods.

4.2.5 The attendance shown in the college system is the official record. Corrections to an attendance
mark must be requested through the course teacher within 7 days of the period concerned.
4.3
Unauthorised
absence
4.3.1 Unauthorised absence (see Rule 2.2.4), including what students call "bunking" a class or a day,
is absence from a period without sanctioned leave or approved on-duty. Each period of unauthorised
absence is marked absent and reduces attendance under section 4.2.
4.3.2 Unauthorised absence on a working day immediately before or after a holiday, or on a Saturday
declared a working day by circular, is reported to the Class Advisor and to the parent or guardian by
SMS on the same day. Three such absences in one semester lead to a written warning under Rule
12.3.1, which is recorded in the student's file.
4.3.3 Unauthorised absence on the day of an Internal Assessment results in zero marks for that
assessment. No re-test is given (see Rule 8.3.1).
4.3.4 There is no allowance of "free" absences. Every period of unauthorised absence counts, even
when the student's attendance remains above 75%.
4.3.5 A student who expects to be absent should apply for leave under Chapter 6 instead of staying
away. Sanctioned leave does not raise the attendance percentage, but it is not reported under Rule
4.3.2 and does not count towards a written warning.
4.4
Condonation
of
attendance
shortage
for
B.Tech
students
4.4.1 A B.Tech student whose attendance in a theory course on the last working day of the semester is
at least 65.0% but below 75.0% may apply for condonation in that course. A student below 65.0%
cannot be condoned and is detained under section 4.5.
4.4.2 Condonation is allowed only for a documented reason: illness supported by medical certificates
submitted under section 6.3, approved on-duty for which the proof was accepted late, or bereavement
in the immediate family. Unauthorised absence is never a reason for condonation.
4.4.3 The condonation fee is payable per course, according to the attendance in that course, as set out
in Table 4.4.

the grade sheet as "detained".
4.5.3 A student detained in three or more courses in one semester must meet the Dean (Academics)
before registering for the next semester.

counselling by the Class Advisor.

This chapter applies only to B.Tech students. The rules for M.Tech students are in Chapter 7. All requests

6.1
Personal
leave
6.1.1 A B.Tech student must apply for personal leave through the college system at least 24 hours
before the leave starts. A request submitted later than this may be rejected by the Class Advisor.1
6.1.2 Personal leave cannot be applied for after the absence (retrospective personal leave is not
allowed). An absence for which personal leave was not applied in time is unauthorised absence
(section 4.3).

8.1
How

8.1.1 Each theory course is assessed out of 100 marks: 40 marks of Continuous Internal Assessment
(CIA) and 60 marks in the End Semester Examination (ESE).
8.1.2 The CIA marks are made up of two Internal Assessments (each conducted for 50 marks and
scaled to 15) and assignments or quizzes for 10 marks.
8.1.3 To pass a course, a student needs at least 50% in the ESE and at least 50% overall. A student
who fails a course has a standing arrear until the course is passed (Rule 2.3.3).
8.2
Internal
Assessments
8.2.1 Each Internal Assessment is a written test of two hours, normally held from 10:00 to 12:00 on
the date shown in the college calendar.
8.2.2 The portions (units) covered by each Internal Assessment are announced by the course teacher
at least one week before the test.
8.2.3 An Internal Assessment may be rescheduled by circular of the Controller of Examinations or the
Head of Department. The circular states the course, the class, the old date and the new date. From the
effective date of the circular, the new date is the date of the assessment for every purpose in this
handbook, including Rule 6.1.4.
8.2.4 The college system shows the current date of each Internal Assessment in the class calendar.
Where the calendar and a later circular differ, the circular prevails (Rule 1.4.3).
8.3
Absence
from
an
Internal
Assessment
8.3.1 A student absent from an Internal Assessment receives zero marks for it. Personal leave is not
granted on Internal Assessment days (Rule 6.1.4), and approved personal leave does not excuse
absence from an assessment rescheduled onto that day (Rule 6.6.2).
8.3.2 A re-test is given only to a student who was hospitalised on the day of the assessment, supported
by a discharge summary, or who was on on-duty approved under Rule 6.4.7. The application for a re-
test must be made within 3 working days of the assessment.
8.3.3 Where an Internal Assessment is rescheduled onto a day on which a student has approved
personal leave, the student should cancel or change the leave in the college system before the new
date (Rule 6.6.2). The Class Advisor may remind affected students but is not required to do so.

acquires a standing arrear after registering is removed from drives that do not allow arrears.

non-dream drive.
10.3.3 A student who has received an offer from a non-dream company may register for dream drives,
but for no further non-dream drives.
10.3.4 A student holding two offers may not register for any further drive.
